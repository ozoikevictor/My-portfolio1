Ten Secrets You Will Not Want To Know

Home / Blog / Ten Secrets

Every developer builds up a private list of habits that quietly separate a good website from a forgettable one. None of these ideas are flashy, and none of them require exotic tools — but skipping them is exactly why so many projects feel almost right instead of genuinely polished.

Most of what follows comes down to restraint: fewer fonts, fewer colors, fewer competing calls to action. Clients rarely ask for less, so it's on the developer to protect the simplicity of the design even when the temptation to add "just one more feature" creeps in.

"Good design is as little design as possible — every detail left in has to earn its place."

The Short List

  • Pick one accent color and let it do all the emphasis work.
  • Set a type scale once and reuse it everywhere, instead of eyeballing sizes per section.
  • Test the mobile nav before the desktop nav — most visitors arrive on a phone.
  • Compress every image before it ships; page weight is a design decision too.
  • Leave generous white space around your call-to-action buttons.

None of these secrets are really secret. They're just easy to skip when a deadline is close, which is exactly why the sites that keep them tend to stand out.
