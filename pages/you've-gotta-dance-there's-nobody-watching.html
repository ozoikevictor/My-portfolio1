You've Gotta Dance Like There's Nobody Watching

Home / Blog / Dance Like Nobody's Watching

Early in a career, most developers copy what's already popular — the same templates, the same layouts, the same "safe" color palette. It's a sensible starting point, but it's also the fastest way to end up with a portfolio that looks like everyone else's.

The projects that get remembered are usually the ones where the developer stopped worrying about what a client or a recruiter might think, and instead built the thing they'd genuinely be proud to show. That confidence is what this post is really about — not literal dancing, obviously, but the same idea: build boldly, and let the polish come from iteration afterward.

"The version of your work that feels a little too personal is usually the version people remember."

How to Actually Do This

  • Start every new project with one deliberately unconventional choice — a color, a layout, a font pairing.
  • Show unfinished work to people you trust before it's "ready."
  • Keep a folder of designs you love that have nothing to do with your own niche.
  • Revisit old projects and note what you'd change — that's your growth, made visible.

None of this means ignoring feedback or best practices. It means putting something real on the page first, then refining it, instead of designing cautiously from the very first pixel.
